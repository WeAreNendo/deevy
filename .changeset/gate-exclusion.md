At a Checkpoint with **Not the Human the work is for** on, that Human can now reject the Gate, in deevy, with `/reject` on the record or with Slack's button; only their approval is refused, and the refusal says they may still reject. Before this, they could do neither. They are still not asked in their Inbox, and the Gate stays off their **Needs me**. A Gate left short of Humans who could approve it, usually because somebody was suspended after its Checkpoint was set, now says so above **Approve** with both numbers, for example "This Gate still wants 2 approvals and only 1 Human can still give one", and says that an admin can lower **Approvals** on the Checkpoint or reinstate a suspended Member. Lowering a Checkpoint's **Approvals**, or removing the Checkpoint so it falls back to one, now passes every open Gate there that already has that many approvals: the Run goes on, the Agent is told, and the record and Slack are updated, all as the admin who saved, saying the threshold was lowered. Turning the exclusion off counts no approval that was refused. For API readers, a Gate (`gates.get`, `gates.list`, `gates.request`, `gates.approve`, `gates.reject`) gains `you.mayApprove`, `you.whyNotApprove` and `shortfall` (`{ wanted, couldApprove, why }`, or null). `you.mayRule` and `you.why` still mean whether you may rule at all and why not, so for the excluded Human `mayRule` is now true and `mayApprove` false. `gates.list({ mine: true })` returns the Gates you could approve, as before. The message refusing the excluded Human's approval now ends "to approve it; you may still reject it". A Gate passed by lowering Approvals has `loweredFrom`, the old threshold, in the payload of its `gate.approved` and `run.answered` Events, which webhooks deliver.
